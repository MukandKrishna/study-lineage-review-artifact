\title{Recognising Study Lineage Is Not Enough: Same-Trial Evidence Over-Credit in Medical LLM Verification}

\author{Anonymous submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract} 

Publication count is not study count: two reports from one clinical trial are not two independent studies. We test whether medical LLM verifiers recognise this distinction and use it when aggregating evidence for a fixed claim. Our source-audited evaluation covers 14 clinical families across two separately frozen suites and five model conditions; it also tests clinical applicability and result direction. Recognition and aggregation are evaluated in separate stateless calls, with repeated calls measuring within-input stability. In the STRIVE example in Figure~\ref{fig:strive-arise-gap}, GPT-5.5 identifies ARISE as the independent trial in 10/10 recognition calls, yet marks the later same-trial STRIVE report as independent corroboration in 5/5 separate aggregation calls. Across the three original provenance families, the same recognition--aggregation contrast holds for GPT-5.5. It also appears in selected Qwen3-4B and Ministral-3-8B family conditions. A study-grouping instruction reduces GPT-5.5 over-credit from 4/5 to 0/5 in each of two later pure-provenance families, but effects in other model conditions are mixed or limited by rejected outputs. These results expose a gap between elicited lineage recognition and evidence aggregation, not an internal mechanism or a universal grouping remedy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A medical verifier can judge a claim against one evidence passage correctly and still mishandle the next passage. The central distinction is simple: a new publication is not necessarily a new study. A second publication may repeat or reanalyse the same underlying trial, while another publication may report an independent trial. Both passages may support the claim, but only the latter represents an additional independent evidential update. Figure~\ref{fig:strive-arise-gap} makes this distinction concrete. It shows two STRIVE reports grouped under one trial, ARISE as an independent trial, and the contrasting GPT-5.5 outcomes on two separate tasks: correct lineage recognition but same-trial over-credit during aggregation. A similar problem arises when positive evidence is clinically credible but falls outside the population or outcome scope of the claim, or when new evidence points in the opposite direction. In each case, the question is not only whether the new passage is relevant, but what kind of update it licenses.

Most factuality and grounding benchmarks frame verification as a static decision over a provided context \citep{min-etal-2023-factscore,hu-etal-2024-knowledge,dammu-etal-2024-claimver}. Citation and RAG evaluations extend this view by asking whether systems retrieve, cite, and synthesize supporting sources \citep{gao-etal-2023-enabling,krishna-etal-2025-fact,es-etal-2024-ragas,saadfalcon-etal-2024-ares}. Recent work has moved closer to dynamic evidence use. GroupQA shows that models may give disproportionate weight to paraphrased duplicates and can be affected by evidence order \citep{naphade-2026-rational}; source-conflict experiments show that repetition can reverse source preferences \citep{schuster-etal-2026-whose}; and belief-revision benchmarks test when a model should update or preserve a conclusion \citep{wilie-etal-2024-belief}. Medical benchmarks likewise expose failures under contextual mismatch and counterfactual evidence \citep{qu-farber-2026-medieval,jullien-etal-2024-semeval}. Together, this literature motivates a more specific test: identifying the source relationship of each added passage and measuring its effect on the combined clinical judgement.

We therefore test evidence updating with two separate tasks. One asks the model to identify how an added report relates to existing evidence; the other asks it to judge the combined evidence. In the three original provenance families, GPT-5.5 identifies the shared lineage in every recognition call yet over-credits the same-trial report in every corresponding aggregation condition (Section~\ref{sec:results}). Selected open-weight conditions show the same family-level contrast. Because the calls are stateless and the recognition answer is not supplied to the verifier, this is an observed gap across tasks, not proof of an internal recognition-then-failure mechanism.

The evaluation keeps each medical claim fixed, adds one source-traceable evidence item at a time, assigns an externally auditable relation label, and scores recognition separately from final judgement integration. Existing work typically addresses static attribution, context faithfulness, source conflict, or belief revision, but rarely isolates clinically meaningful update relations under source-level controls. Beyond study provenance, our families test whether evidence applies to the claim's clinical scope and whether its result supports or challenges the claim.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We construct two source-audited suites totalling 14 clinical families across study provenance, clinical applicability, and result direction, with null controls and counterbalanced recognition inputs. The suites were frozen at different stages, and rejected model outputs remain visible.
    \item We show a family-level recognition--aggregation gap: GPT-5.5 correctly labels lineage but over-credits same-trial reports in separate calls in all three original provenance families; selected open-weight conditions show the same contrast.
    \item We test an explicit study-grouping instruction against document-level aggregation. It reduces GPT-5.5 over-credit in two later pure-provenance families, while independent-trial support and model-dependent failures are reported separately.
\end{itemize}


\section{Related Work}

\subsection{From pointwise factuality to diagnostic evaluation}

Fine-grained factuality work has shown that the unit of evaluation matters. FActScore decomposes long-form generations into atomic claims \citep{min-etal-2023-factscore}; RefChecker and ClaimVer localize attribution, contradiction, and unsupported content at finer granularity \citep{hu-etal-2024-knowledge,dammu-etal-2024-claimver}. ALCE evaluates cited generation along correctness and citation-quality axes, while FRAMES evaluates retrieval, factuality, and multi-hop synthesis in end-to-end RAG \citep{gao-etal-2023-enabling,krishna-etal-2025-fact}. RAGAS and ARES separate dimensions such as faithfulness, relevance, and evaluator reliability \citep{es-etal-2024-ragas,saadfalcon-etal-2024-ares}. Sufficient Context makes another useful separation: whether the retrieved context contains enough information and whether the model actually uses it \citep{joren-etal-2025-sufficient}. We extend this diagnostic approach to controlled additions of evidence for a fixed medical claim.

Recent strong evaluation papers follow a similar methodological pattern: replace a broad endpoint with a smaller set of interpretable subproblems. Atomic NLI decomposition reveals inconsistency hidden by high overall NLI accuracy \citep{srikanth-rudinger-2025-nli}; MediEval separates medical truth from patient-context support in a factorial design and names the resulting failure modes \citep{qu-farber-2026-medieval}. This motivates our own decomposition of evidence updating into relation recognition and integration.

\subsection{Conflict, repetition, and belief updating}

Controlled evidence studies show that LLMs are sensitive to factors that should not be treated as independent evidence. GroupQA reports that duplicated paraphrases can shift answers even when they do not add genuinely new support, and that evidence order also matters \citep{naphade-2026-rational}. Whose Facts Win? shows source preferences under inter-context conflict, but also that repetition can reverse those preferences \citep{schuster-etal-2026-whose}. ECON separates inter-evidence conflict detection from conflict resolution, finding that models may identify conflict yet still resolve it by favouring one source or falling back on prior knowledge \citep{jiayang-etal-2024-econ}. Belief-R evaluates whether models update when premises change and preserve conclusions when they should remain stable \citep{wilie-etal-2024-belief}. When Facts Change further separates temporal-conflict detection from recognizing fact mutability and from acting on that judgement; larger models can identify the relevant temporal relation yet fail to propagate it to the final prediction \citep{wallat-etal-2026-facts}.

Work on context faithfulness shows that models' use of supplied evidence depends on memory strength, evidence presentation, and task requirements \citep{li-etal-2025-investigating,sun-etal-2026-task}. We examine the specific case of source dependence in clinical evidence. Two publications can agree with the same medical claim while arising from one randomized trial or from independent trials. 
We test whether a verifier uses that distinction when it combines their findings. We also test whether clinically credible evidence is applied within the population and outcome scope of the claim.

Repeated judge calls also require care. Rating Roulette reports substantial intra-rater inconsistency in LLM-as-a-judge scoring \citep{haldar-hockenmaier-2025-rating}. We therefore use repeats to estimate within-input stability, not as independent samples, and treat the clinical family as the experimental unit.

\subsection{Internal representations and downstream use}

ProbeRAG shows that aligned and conflicting RAG states can be separated in hidden representations and uses a learned conflict probe as part of a faithfulness intervention \citep{gao-etal-2026-beyond-black}. SpARE similarly finds signals of conflict between context and memory in intermediate activations and uses sparse-autoencoder features to steer knowledge selection \citep{zhao-etal-2025-steering}. These studies motivate future representation-level work on study dependence. The present evaluation measures observable relation recognition and evidence use.

The confidence-updating studies of \citet{kumaran-etal-2026-competing,kumaran-etal-2026-causal} illustrate a useful sequence of diagnostic experiments. One manipulates answer visibility and the direction of advice to measure sensitivity to new evidence. The other measures confidence before abstention is available, compares it with alternative predictors, and tests confidence-related activations and decision thresholds. We use the same logic to isolate one explanation for evidence-update behavior at a time.

BayesBench likewise separates latent-state inference from downstream prediction under sequential evidence accumulation \citep{samanta-etal-2026-bayesbench}, while Contextual Belief Management distinguishes failed stay, failed update, and failed isolation in a closed-world belief-tracking setting \citep{xu-etal-2026-cbm}.

Clinical evidence provides an additional warning against overinterpreting a successful probe. In one recent study, activation probes recovered evidence-grade information more accurately than models' explicit grade statements, but the analyses also showed strong dependence on surface wording and weak transfer across topics and grading rubrics \citep{tayebi-arasteh-2026-strength}. Any provenance probe in our setting must therefore use topic-held-out splits and explicit lexical/metadata controls.

\subsection{Medical evidence and contextual grounding}

Clinical trial NLI offers source-linked evidence and controlled perturbations \citep{jullien-etal-2024-semeval}. MediEval separates world-level medical truth from support in the patient context, showing why a medically true statement can still be unsupported in the case at hand \citep{qu-farber-2026-medieval}. Recent evidence-grounded science and scholarly-QA work also emphasizes source traceability and provenance interfaces \citep{tan-etal-2026-scitrue,martinboyle-etal-2026-papertrail}. For medical communication, \citet{wong-etal-2025-dangerous} show that source-grounded answers may remain locally accurate yet mislead users when they strip evidence from its original context or purpose. FORCEBENCH frames a related risk for cited RAG: topical relevance alone does not guarantee that a citation licenses the strength, scope, or specificity of the generated claim \citep{qian-etal-2026-relevant}. Our source-audited families test how a verifier treats a later report from an existing trial compared with a report from an independent trial.

Together, these studies motivate our operational question. For a fixed medical claim and evidence state, we identify the relation of each new source-grounded item and test whether the verifier uses that relation in its combined judgement.

\section{Research Questions}
\label{sec:rq}

We ask three questions about study dependence in evidence aggregation.

\paragraph{RQ1: Lineage recognition.}
Can a model identify whether two publications report the same underlying study or independent studies?

\paragraph{RQ2: Recognition and aggregation.}
When separate recognition calls identify shared study lineage correctly, do aggregation calls nevertheless credit the later report as independent corroboration?

\paragraph{RQ3: Study-level grouping.}
Does an explicit study-grouping instruction reduce same-trial over-credit while preserving corroboration from a genuinely independent study?

\section{Evidence-Update Reliability}
\label{sec:task}

We evaluate how medical claim verifiers use changes in supplied evidence. Each source-audited evidence state has a fixed claim and a known update relation established before model inference. We test whether the verifier recognizes the relation and respects it in the combined judgement.
\subsection{Evidence states and relations}
Let $c$ denote the medical claim under evaluation. An initial evidence state $E_0=\{e_1,\ldots,e_k\}$ is accepted only when it already contains enough source-grounded information for the intended baseline judgement. An update $u$ is then added to form $E_1=E_0\cup\{u\}$. We test three relations between $u$, $c$, and the existing evidence state:

\paragraph{Provenance.} Is $u$ genuinely independent evidence, another report from the same underlying study, or an exact repeat? Study identity is checked against preserved publication metadata and trial identifiers where available.

\paragraph{Applicability.} Does $u$ apply to the claim as written with respect to population, intervention, comparator, outcome, and time? Evidence can be credible and topically relevant while still falling outside the claim scope.

\paragraph{Direction.} Does $u$ agree with, challenge, or leave unclear the claim-relevant result? Direction is defined from the observed claim-relevant result rather than inferred from statistical significance alone.

Each relation has a distinct expected effect. Exact repeats and same-study reports preserve the count of independent studies. The scoped judgement follows applicable evidence; an out-of-scope item contributes no claim-level support. Applicable opposing evidence registers conflict while retaining earlier support. Independent supporting evidence may add corroboration. Numerical warrant scores serve as secondary diagnostics.

\subsection{Recognition versus integration}

We evaluate two abilities in separate stateless calls. \emph{Relation recognition} asks whether the model identifies the relevant relation without producing a final verdict. \emph{Integration} asks whether the verifier respects that relation in its combined judgement. Correct recognition alongside incorrect aggregation is the behavioural contrast of interest. The recognition response is never supplied to the aggregation call, so this contrast does not identify an internal mechanism or measure the effect of passing a recognition answer to the verifier.

\subsection{Measurement boundary}

Categorical relation labels and relation-specific integration behaviour are the primary outcomes. Numerical support, warrant, conflict, and confidence scores are retained as diagnostics. Small score movements are not treated as violations unless they exceed variation established by an appropriate development control. Repetitions estimate within-input stability; they are not independent clinical observations.

\section{Controlled Clinical Families}
\label{sec:families}

\subsection{Construction and acceptance checks}

Each family contains one fixed claim, an initial evidence set, a matched update pair that isolates a declared relation, and a null control. Before any model call, we record the expected relation and the expected categorical integration behaviour. A family is rejected if the update fills a missing semantic bridge in the baseline, changes several clinically material dimensions at once, or lacks an externally checkable source relation.

The pre-call audit checks population, intervention, comparator, outcome, time, observed direction, study identity, exact source text, and whether the null genuinely adds no information. Development work showed that an apparent provenance effect could disappear once semantic novelty was removed. This audit makes construct validity part of the experimental method.

\subsection{Prompts and counterbalancing}

The neutral verifier receives only the fixed claim and the complete evidence state. It labels each evidence item and produces a combined judgement without being told which relation is under test. A separate recognition prompt compares Update A and Update B for one target relation only. Both A/B and B/A orders are run. Gold labels, expected score movement, and construction notes are never exposed to the model. Appendix~\ref{app:prompt-contract} reproduces the core prompt templates and output contract.

Each evidence condition is a fresh, stateless input. This choice controls for the effect of showing the model its previous answer, as in controlled change-of-mind studies \citep{kumaran-etal-2026-competing}. A later secondary study could compare shown and hidden prior verdicts to quantify self-anchoring.

\subsection{Primary metrics}

We report relation-recognition accuracy, order consistency, appropriate-integration rate, null stability, and relation-specific failures such as redundancy inflation, scope contamination, and conflict omission. For the recognition--use contrast, we identify families with correct recognition in both counterbalanced orders and inspect valid aggregation calls for those same families. This is a family-level comparison across separate stateless tasks, not a conditional probability over paired calls.

\section{Experiments}

\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental sequence}

We organise the evaluation as a sequence of primary and follow-up tests. Each clinical family is first constructed and source-audited as described in Section~\ref{sec:families}. The claim, evidence passages, relation label, and expected categorical behaviour are frozen before model inference. We then test \emph{integration} with the neutral verifier and \emph{recognition} with a counterbalanced comparison task. A held-out compositional family places provenance, applicability, and direction updates around the same claim. Follow-up diagnostics are introduced only after a repeatable primary-suite failure: oracle conditions test whether explicit relation information or an explicit marginal-evidence policy is sufficient for repair, while a cue ablation removes the study-group label used in this evaluation from H3. This ordering keeps the primary behavioural evaluation separate from interventions designed to test narrower explanations.

For clarity, we distinguish four study-design components. The original ten-family suite is the prospectively frozen primary confirmatory evaluation of recognition and integration, with the held-out H3 family testing composition. The later P5R/P6R/H5/H6 package is a separately frozen follow-up evaluation of study grouping and independent-trial preservation; P5R/P6R also support a secondary two-prompt robustness audit. The CF-P3 oracle tests and H3-M1 cue ablation are post-hoc diagnostics introduced after the primary provenance failure was observed; they are outside the primary denominator. The lineage-by-overlap family is exploratory and policy-instructed, and is reported only as a control rather than as an unprompted lineage-discovery result.

\subsection{Evaluation suite}

The original ten-family evaluation contains three provenance families (CF-P1--P3: migraine, severe asthma, and chronic kidney disease), three applicability families (CF-A1--A3: melanoma, atrial-fibrillation stroke prevention, and ovarian-cancer maintenance), three direction families (CF-D1--D3: glucose control in intensive care, glycaemic control in type 2 diabetes, and vitamin-D fall prevention), and the held-out compositional statin family H3. The primitive families isolate one relation at a time; H3 combines provenance, applicability, and direction updates around one claim. A separately frozen later package adds two provenance families (P5R and P6R) and two compositional families (H5 and H6). Appendix Table~\ref{tab:family-inventory} lists the selected family targets and source identities. The clinical family is the experimental unit; repeated invocations measure stability.

For all nine primitive confirmatory families, we remove the study-group label used in this evaluation (\texttt{source\_group\_id}) from model-visible inputs while retaining truthful source titles, publication identifiers, trial identifiers, source locators, and passages. The original H3 baseline retained this field and serves as a cue-rich compositional reference. H3-M1 is a diagnostic ablation of H3 that removes \texttt{source\_group\_id} while preserving the claim, evidence order, passages, and remaining source metadata. The contrast measures dependence on the study-group label while bibliographic and structured \texttt{trial\_id} cues remain available.

\subsection{Model and execution}

We evaluate five recorded model conditions: \texttt{gpt-5.5} at medium reasoning effort, \texttt{Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507}, \texttt{Gemma-3-4B-it}, \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}, and \texttt{Ministral-3-8B-Instruct-2512}. The Codex interface leaves GPT temperature and random seed unspecified; open-weight models use their recorded decoding and chat-template adapters (Appendix~\ref{app:prompt-contract}). Five fresh, stateless invocations per exact input measure within-input stability. We retain prompt templates, response schemas, configurations, frozen inputs, rendered prompts, source-audit records, raw and parsed outputs, and content hashes. Valid and rejected outputs are counted separately.

The open-weight checkpoints are the nominal 4B variants for Qwen3 and Gemma3 and the nominal 8B variants for Llama 3.1 and Ministral 3, following their model names. The recorded open-weight runs were executed in Kaggle GPU sessions configured with two Tesla T4 GPUs, each showing a maximum of 15~GiB of GPU memory. Qwen3 and Gemma3 loaded on \texttt{cuda:0}; Llama and Ministral were sharded across both GPUs. Qwen3 and Gemma3 used Python 3.12.13, PyTorch 2.10.0+cu128, and Transformers 5.0.0; Llama and Ministral used Python 3.13.15, PyTorch 2.11.0+cu128, and Transformers 5.16.1. The archived run records do not include reliable wall-clock or GPU-hour totals, so we do not report them. GPT-5.5's parameter count and hardware were not recorded in the run records.

The open-weight checkpoints were loaded from the Hugging Face Hub; the exact repository identifiers and pinned revisions are listed in \texttt{configs/transformers\_gpu\_models.json}.

The cross-model runs preserve the clinical claims, evidence passages, gold behaviours, and semantic task instructions within each input version while adapting model-specific formatting. The ten-family and later four-family packages were frozen and run at different stages; they were not one simultaneous prospective construction. The selected P6R inputs include a later source-corrected independent passage, as documented in Appendix~\ref{app:prompt-contract}.

Each primitive family contributes six exact inputs: one initial state, three update conditions, and two counterbalanced recognition conditions. Five repetitions per input yield 30 responses per family and 270 responses across the nine families. H3 contributes 12 exact inputs: one initial state, five update conditions, and six relation-recognition conditions. These add 60 responses, bringing the original suite to 66 exact inputs and 330 planned calls per model. The later four-family package adds 50 recognition, 85 document-level, and 85 study-grouped calls per model. Thus the selected 14-family comparison accounts for 550 calls per model; completion does not imply that every output passed the scientific validity checks. We keep the accounting separate: the original suite has 1,650 planned calls across five models, the later package has 1,100, prompt robustness has 120 across three models, oracle and cue diagnostics have 195 across three models, and the exploratory lineage-overlap control has 36 calls.

\subsection{Controls and diagnostic interventions}

The exact-repeat condition tests whether merely adding or repeating text changes the judgement. Within provenance families, the same-trial and independent-study updates separate publication count from independent-study count. Within applicability families, positive out-of-scope updates test whether topical similarity or favourable direction is sufficient to influence a narrower claim. Within direction families, applicable challenging updates test whether the verifier mechanically strengthens its judgement whenever evidence is added. Counterbalanced recognition orders reduce an option-position explanation. H3 tests the further alternative that the update policy works only when each relation is isolated in a separate family.

Oracle and cue-ablation diagnostics followed the primary confirmatory suite. CF-P3 was selected post hoc after the primary analysis found a repeatable provenance-integration error despite correct comparative recognition. The diagnostic conditions were frozen before their model calls. The \emph{relation oracle} supplies the item-specific study-dependence relation, leaving the final verdict to the model. The \emph{policy oracle} supplies a general rule: repeated or same-trial reports may add detail while contributing no independent corroboration. Each oracle uses the exact-repeat, same-trial, and independent-study conditions, adding six exact inputs and 30 responses in total.

H3-M1 contributes two counterbalanced provenance-recognition inputs and five neutral-update inputs. It reuses the H3 initial state, which lacks the removed field. H3-M1 therefore adds seven exact inputs and 35 responses. Across CF-P3 and H3-M1, the diagnostic design contains 13 exact inputs and 65 responses \emph{per tested model}, outside both the 330-call original-suite and 550-call selected-comparison denominators. GPT-5.5, Qwen3-4B, and Gemma3-4B completed this diagnostic design; Llama and Ministral did not. Completion is distinct from output validity, particularly for Gemma. These interventions measure how explicit relation identity, an explicit update rule, and removal of a study-group cue affect observable behaviour.

\subsection{Scope of inference}

The two evaluation packages cover 14 clinical families under five recorded model conditions. Families and clinical contrasts define their breadth; repeated invocations measure within-input stability. Comparative recognition measures whether a model can label a relation when asked directly, not whether its answer is carried into a later aggregation call. H3 tests composition with a study-group cue, and H3-M1 measures the effect of removing that cue while retaining bibliographic and trial identity information. Oracle tests measure sensitivity to explicit relation or policy information. The later study-grouping prompt is an intervention, not external deduplication; the P5R/P6R prompt-order robustness audit and the one-family lineage-by-overlap control are reported separately from the main evaluation \citep{mizrahi-etal-2024-state,ceballos-arroyo-etal-2024-open}.

\paragraph{Code and data availability.} The anonymized repository is available at \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/study-lineage-review-artifact/}. It contains the paper source, frozen inputs, rendered prompts, schemas, configurations, recorded outputs, manifests, audit summaries, figures, and rerun scripts. GPT-5.5 calls were run through Codex with medium reasoning effort; the archived records support audit of those calls, while exact GPT reruns require Codex access. The open-weight runs can be rerun with the pinned model revisions and GPU runner.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The first three Results subsections answer RQ1, RQ2, and RQ3, respectively; the fourth reports contrast conditions for applicability, result direction, and composition. The later subsections report secondary robustness, exploratory-control, and post-hoc diagnostic analyses; they do not redefine the primary three-question evaluation.

\subsection{Can models recognise study lineage?}

The original provenance families CF-P1--P3 each ask the model to compare a same-trial report with an independent trial in both A/B orders. \subsection{Does recognised lineage constrain aggregation?}

In each of CF-P1, CF-P2, and CF-P3, GPT-5.5 recognises study lineage in 10/10 comparative calls but assigns independent corroboration to the same-trial update in 5/5 separate aggregation calls. The finding therefore occurs in three of three original provenance families; 15/15 describes stability across calls, not 15 independent clinical cases. Qwen shows the same contrast in CF-P1 and CF-P3. Its CF-P2 aggregation has five rejected outputs, so that family cannot support a semantic over-credit estimate. Ministral shows the contrast in CF-P1; Gemma and Llama do not provide complete correct recognition across both orders in any of these three families. In the later pure-provenance conditions, GPT-5.5 again recognises P5R and P6R in 10/10 calls each and over-credits the same-trial update in 4/5 document-level calls per family. These comparisons show a behavioural gap across tasks, not that a recognition answer was ignored within a shared model state.

\subsection{Can study grouping reduce over-credit while preserving independent evidence?}

The later package compares a neutral document-level verifier with an explicit study-grouping instruction on the same family conditions. Figure~\ref{fig:grouping-results} shows the attributable same-trial over-credit comparison in the two pure-provenance families. In P5R and P6R, GPT-5.5's over-credit falls from 4/5 to 0/5 in each family; Ministral improves in P6R. Qwen and Gemma remain at 5/5 in both families; Llama is already at zero in its one fully valid comparison. The remaining comparisons have rejected outputs in at least one mode. This is a family-level, model-dependent effect of a prompting intervention, not external study deduplication. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:compositional-detail} reports the compositional H5/H6 flags separately: both initial states already contain an independent study, so a change in the global flag cannot isolate credit given to the added same-trial report.

\subsection{Do other evidence relations show the same pattern?}

The applicability and direction families provide contrast conditions, not additional lineage tests. GPT-5.5 selects the target update in all six families under both A/B orders (10/10 recognition calls per family). In its separate verifier calls, it ignores the positive but out-of-scope update in each CF-A family (5/5 per family) and treats the challenging update as conflicting or mixed evidence in each CF-D family (5/5 per family). Qwen recognises the target update in 10/10 calls in each CF-A family, but its applicable CF-A3 update has five rejected verifier outputs; its CF-D recognition varies by family and order. Thus the provenance gap is not a uniform failure on every tested relation, but these selected controls do not establish general applicability or direction reliability. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:control-axis-recognition} gives recognition and rejection denominators for all five models.

In the held-out compositional H3 family, GPT-5.5 and Qwen each get 30/30 recognition calls correct across provenance, applicability, and direction comparisons. Nevertheless, their separate verifier calls flag the added same-trial WOSCOPS report as independent corroboration in 4/5 and 5/5 valid calls, respectively. H3 extends the observed recognition--aggregation contrast beyond a primitive provenance-only family; the 30 calls cover three relation types, so they are not 30 lineage comparisons.

\subsection{Does the provenance failure survive a prompt-order change?}

A secondary prompt-robustness audit reran the P5R and P6R document-level conditions with a semantically equivalent reordered verifier prompt. It comprised 40 calls per model for GPT-5.5, Qwen3-4B, and Gemma3-4B, covering two families, same-trial and independent updates, two prompt variants, and five repetitions. GPT-5.5 marked same-trial updates as independent in 16/20 records and passed the independent-update semantic audit in 14/20. Qwen marked every same-trial record as independent (20/20); its independent-update audit passed 15/20 because five records flagged independent corroboration without using the added evidence. Gemma was canonically parseable in all 40 records but strict-valid in 0/40 because every output had one outer Markdown JSON fence; its same-trial records failed the provenance flag rule in 20/20, while its independent-update audit passed 20/20. Thus the broad failure survived the prompt-order change, but the exact failure form and format validity were model-dependent. These secondary calls show robustness across the tested prompt variants only; they do not establish full prompt invariance and are not pooled with the primary denominators. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:prompt-robustness} gives the audit counts.

Across the selected 14-family comparison, each model has 550 accounted calls. Canonically valid/rejected outputs are 550/0 for GPT-5.5, 480/70 for Qwen, 400/150 for Gemma, 395/155 for Llama, and 410/140 for Ministral; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:family-validity} gives the family-level breakdown. Semantic rates above use valid outputs only; a rejected output is neither a correct nor an incorrect scientific judgement. The five repetitions document within-input stability rather than increasing the number of clinical cases.

\subsection{Exploratory lineage-by-overlap control}

The paper reports one ProMISe/ARISE exploratory, policy-instructed control crossing same-trial versus independent-trial evidence with high versus low textual overlap. Its passage and overlap criteria were fixed before the model calls. Under a prompt that explicitly defines same-trial reports as non-independent and separate trials as independent, GPT-5.5 gives the expected combination label in all four cells across 20/20 calls. Qwen gives the expected label in three of four cells; Gemma succeeds in the two independent-trial cells but not the same-trial cells. Llama's four valid cell outputs are mixed, and Ministral has two valid and two rejected cell outputs. This is a one-family test of applying a stated lineage policy across overlap strata. It is not an unprompted lineage-discovery test or a causal estimate of the effect of overlap.

The lexical and semantic overlap values and source-faithful passage construction are reported in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:overlap-construction}; the policy-defined four-cell result is in Table~\ref{tab:overlap-policy-results}. The exact-repeat, null, same-trial, and independent-update controls are summarized separately in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:control-outcomes}; applicability and direction integration are broken down by family in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:integration-controls}.

\subsection{Follow-up oracle and cue diagnostics}

On CF-P3, GPT-5.5's neutral verifier over-credits the same-trial report in 5/5 calls. Supplying either the item-specific relation or a general study-dependence policy removes that flag in all five calls while retaining independent-trial corroboration in 5/5. Qwen still over-credits the same-trial report in 5/5 calls under each oracle. Gemma's same-trial oracle outputs are rejected, so no semantic repair estimate is available. In H3-M1, removing the study-group label (\texttt{source\_group\_id}) leaves GPT-5.5's provenance recognition correct in both orders (10/10), yet its separate verifier still flags the same-trial report as independent in 5/5 calls. Qwen and Gemma each answer only one of the two recognition orders correctly (5/10) and also flag the same-trial report in 5/5 valid verifier calls. These post hoc diagnostics show model-dependent prompt sensitivity; they do not establish a shared internal mechanism or cue-free lineage inference. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:diagnostic-results} gives condition-level denominators and rejection boundaries.

\section{Conclusion}

Across source-audited clinical families, correct answers to a study-lineage question do not ensure that a separate verifier call will respect that lineage when aggregating reports. The clearest contrast occurs in three original provenance families for GPT-5.5 and in selected conditions for other models. Explicit study grouping reduces GPT-5.5 same-trial over-credit in two later pure-provenance families, but is not a general repair across models. Clinical families, rather than repeated calls, define the scope of these findings. Because recognition and aggregation were separate stateless tasks, the results identify a behavioural gap, not its internal cause.

\section*{Limitations}

The 14 source-audited families are selected clinical contrasts, not a representative sample of medical claims or publications. The original ten-family and later four-family suites were frozen at different stages, and some later selected inputs use source-corrected passages. Five repetitions of an exact input measure within-input stability, not five independent cases; we do not infer population-level error rates from pooled calls. Recognition and aggregation are separate stateless tasks, so their disagreement does not establish what the model internally represented during aggregation. The grouping condition changes the prompt and exposes study identifiers and a study-group cue; it is not external deduplication and its effect need not transfer to settings without those cues. The prompt-robustness audit covers only P5R/P6R, two prompt variants, and three models; Gemma's canonical parsing does not remove its strict-format limitation. Some open-weight outputs were rejected by the response contract, limiting semantic comparisons. H5/H6 global corroboration flags cannot isolate the added same-trial report, and the lineage-by-overlap control covers only one policy-instructed family. These boundaries limit generalisation and causal interpretation.

\section*{Ethical considerations}

This is an evaluation of model behaviour on source-linked clinical evidence, not a system for diagnosis or treatment decisions. Over-crediting repeated reports could overstate the strength of clinical support, while rejecting or suppressing genuinely independent evidence could hide useful findings. We therefore distinguish same-trial reports from independent studies, report invalid outputs separately from scientific errors, and avoid presenting tested claims as current clinical recommendations. The repository contains derived research records and source identifiers, not model weights, credentials, or patient-level personal data. Models remain subject to their original access terms, and the clinical sources remain subject to their publication and reuse terms. Any clinical use would require independent source verification and qualified human review.

\section{Prompt and scoring contract}
\label{app:prompt-contract}

Figures~\ref{fig:recognition-prompt}--\ref{fig:grouped-prompt} reproduce the core templates: comparative recognition (v2.2), document-level verification (v2), and study-grouped verification (v1). These short names correspond to the full filenames and hashes in the frozen run configurations. The literal \texttt{\{model\_input\_json\}} placeholder is replaced, once per call, by the frozen JSON input containing the claim, displayed source metadata, and evidence passages. Gold answers and expected score movements are not in that input. The figures show the base templates; they omit the varying clinical JSON payload and the model-specific JSON-schema formatting suffix described below. Recognition answers are never passed to verifier calls.

\subsection{Inputs, output validation, and execution}
\label{app:prompt-execution}

The recognition input contains \texttt{claim\_text}, \texttt{target\_relation}, \texttt{initial\_evidence}, \texttt{update\_a}, and \texttt{update\_b}; verifier inputs contain the fixed claim, \texttt{initial\_evidence}, and either an \texttt{added\_evidence} object or null. Each displayed evidence object carries an evidence ID, ordered bundle position, source title, passage, and available source identifiers. Some original-suite conditions omit the study-group label (\texttt{source\_group\_id}); the later grouped condition includes it and \texttt{trial\_id}. This is a prompt-and-cue intervention, not externally deduplicated evidence.

Recognition responses must contain the item and target relation, an A/B/no-difference answer, confidence, metadata basis, exact passage quotations, and a reason. Verifier responses must assess every displayed item in order and return an overall label, scores, used/ignored evidence IDs, combination flags, and a reason. The reported over-credit measure reads the \texttt{independent\_corroboration} flag on the same-trial-update condition. We validate the JSON schema, item identity, evidence coverage and order, and quoted substrings before semantic scoring; rejected outputs remain in the denominator accounting but receive no semantic score.

GPT-5.5 ran through Codex with medium reasoning effort; that interface did not specify temperature or random seed. The open-weight runs used each model's chat template and greedy decoding (\texttt{do\_sample=false}, \texttt{max\_new\_tokens=2048}). In the combined later-package Qwen/Gemma runner, a provider adapter appended a compact JSON Schema as a format contract to the base template; other model/run adapters differed and are retained in their archived configurations. These formatting instructions did not add clinical evidence or a gold answer. Raw output, strict-format validity, canonical validity after removal of an optional outer JSON fence, and validation errors were retained separately. Five fresh invocations per exact input assess response stability, not five independent clinical cases. Frozen run configurations record model revisions, rendered-prompt and input hashes, and the selected source passages.

For P6R, the selected 50 calls per model comprise 30 unchanged calls and 20 later calls using the source-reviewed independent passage for recognition and independent-update conditions. The superseded and selected inputs are not pooled. This compact provenance note preserves the reproducible selection without treating P6R as a distinct experimental design.

\subsection{Lineage-by-overlap construction and results}
\label{app:overlap-control-detail}

The exploratory control uses one source-reviewed ProMISe/ARISE family. The fixed claim concerns 90-day all-cause mortality for early septic shock under early goal-directed therapy (EGDT) versus usual resuscitation. The initial anchor is the ProMISe primary report (\texttt{ISRCTN36307479}); the same-trial update is the ProMISe HTA report with the same registry identifier; the independent update is the ARISE primary report (\texttt{NCT00975793}). The four passages are source-faithful result or conclusion passages: two from the ProMISe lineage and two from ARISE. Review checks recorded the registry linkage, population, intervention, comparator, outcome, time frame, source locator, exact passage text, and passage hashes. No passage was synthetically paraphrased, trimmed, or padded, and the model-visible input did not contain the study-group label (\texttt{source\_group\_id}). We report this as an exploratory, policy-instructed control outside the confirmatory denominator; it is not a confirmatory or causal overlap experiment.

Overlap was measured against the fixed initial ProMISe numerical-result anchor using lowercase Unicode-normalized word/number tokens with punctuation removed. We computed token-set Jaccard, ROUGE-L F1 on the same token sequence, and cosine similarity from the pinned \texttt{sentence-transformers/all-mpnet-base-v2} revision \texttt{e8c3b32e\allowbreak df5434bc\allowbreak 2275fc9b\allowbreak ab85f826\allowbreak 40a19130}. Within each lineage, the high passage had to exceed the low passage on all three measures, with a semantic-cosine gap of at least 0.10 and a lexical gap of at least 0.10 on Jaccard or ROUGE-L. Same-high and independent-high, and likewise same-low and independent-low, were additionally required to be close on semantic and at least one lexical measure. We use these thresholds to define high- and low-overlap strata; they are not a causal manipulation of overlap.
